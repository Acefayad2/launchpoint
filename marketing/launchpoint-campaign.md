# LaunchPoint — Build your next chapter

Status: campaign proposal for discussion. Scripts and production briefs are prepared; no media has been generated and no paid campaign has been launched.

## Positioning

**Build your next chapter. Give it a LaunchPoint.**

LaunchPoint builds custom software, websites, and applications, with marketing and social media management to help businesses reach customers.

Organize the offer into two connected needs:
- **Build:** websites, web and mobile applications, client portals, internal tools, and integrations.
- **Grow:** marketing campaigns, content, social media management, and ongoing improvement.

Each acquisition ad should sell one useful next step. Avoid listing every service in every ad. Use the broader message on the website and in brand creative.

## First campaign hypothesis

Start with local service businesses that have an outdated website or struggle to post consistently. This is a proposed audience based on the existing site, pending owner input. Run founder/app-development creative separately: the buyer, proof, and buying cycle differ.

Offer: the existing free growth audit, framed as a conversation about the business's website and marketing priorities. Software leads get a project discovery conversation. Do not promise a free complete strategy, fixed delivery date, or guaranteed growth.

CTA: **Request your free growth audit.**
Destination: the verified LaunchPoint production page, ending in `#contact`. Confirm its domain before publishing an ad.

Purpose: sell a website project to a local service business.

Headline: Give your website a LaunchPoint.
Hook variants: “Would you contact your own business through this website?” / “Great business. Outdated website?”

Use a consistent reference character and location for subsequent shots.

Production: generate the owner/location footage in Higgsfield; capture the website demo directly. Composite screen content and add typography in editing so navigation and brand text remain accurate. Label fictional interface comparisons as illustrative demos.

## Creative direction

Retain the site's dark navy and orange identity. Use real work, real interfaces, and readable text. Prefer one controlled camera move per shot. The editing should feel purposeful and human; keep effects secondary to the service being sold.

Start with Ad 1: it has a clear visual comparison and is easier to substantiate with a real website example. Ad 2 is the strongest alternative if recurring social management is the business priority. Ad 3 needs a credible software demo before production.

Higgsfield's official camera-controls page lists moves such as Dolly In and Handheld: https://higgsfield.ai/camera-controls. These briefs are creative directions, not executed presets or guarantees of available models, credits, or account features. Confirm available tools when producing the chosen ad.

## Small test plan

1. Finish one concept with two opening hooks and the same body/CTA. Keep audience and landing destination consistent to make the hook comparison useful.
2. Initially share as organic posts, or propose a paid pilot after budget and target geography are chosen. No ad spend is authorized by this document.
3. Proposed paid pilot, if approved: $300 total over 10 days, split evenly between two hook variations. This is a planning example, not a performance forecast; generation costs and management fees are separate. Assess whether likely lead costs make that sample meaningful before spending.
4. Review at days 3, 7, and 10. Record impressions, landing-page visits, valid inquiries, qualified inquiries, booked conversations, and proposals. Treat small samples as directional; do not pick winners on views alone.
5. Define a qualified inquiry as a real business/project, a need aligned with LaunchPoint's services, and a plausible timeline and budget established in follow-up. Agree an acceptable cost per qualified inquiry before a paid test. Fix tracking or obvious delivery issues before scaling.
